DS Team Consults SAP

Your proposal enters a structured review — from DS evaluation through to an official SAP decision.

💡 If it's urgent, build a local placeholder component, keep it isolated, and mark it as pending DS review so it can be swapped cleanly once a decision lands.

1 DS Evaluates

We review scope, precedent, and alignment with SAP UI5 direction.

2 SAP Escalation

If needed, we escalate to the SAP UI5 working group for an official decision.

3 Outcome

We'll reach out with the decision — usually within 2–4 weeks.

Was Your Proposal Accepted?

Proposal Not Accepted — What Now?

A rejection means not yet, not never. The steps below help you understand why and come back with a stronger case.

1 Understand the Why

Ask why it was declined — scope, precedent, timing, or an existing solution that already covers it.

2 Re-Frame and Re-Propose

A sprint blocker or wider team impact carries more weight. If context changed, bring it back to #design-system-stack.

3 Build Locally as a Last Resort

This is your last option, not a shortcut. Use DS tokens and patterns, document everything, and plan to replace it.

Would Other Teams Need It?

If at least 2 other teams would use it, it belongs in the design system.

Extension to LeanIX Library

How your component gets contributed to Team Gaia's library.

1 Align with Team Gaia

Work with Team Gaia to align on design tokens and API surface.

2 Submit for Review

Submit a design review in Figma and a code implementation for review.

3 Ship in the Library

Once accepted, it ships in the LeanIX component library — credit to your team.

Build It Locally but Future-Friendly

No shared component exists yet. This is the exception, not the rule. Follow these steps to keep the door open for contribution later.

1 Name It Clearly

Name it local and temporary. If your team doesn't know its status, it will be treated as permanent.

2 Follow DS Patterns

Use design tokens and UI5 API patterns. No custom styles, no shortcuts. It must be replaceable.

3 Document the Why

Document the use case and why it's not shared yet. If you skip this, the next team will build it again.
